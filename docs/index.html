Your hybrid‑work attendance, audited nightly by an app that's actually on your side.

Office Ledger counts your days present against your eligible working days — leave, public holidays and comp‑off excluded, exactly the way the policy says it should — so you know where you stand before it comes up in your 1:1.

01

Why this exists

ANZ's hybrid‑work policy runs on a percentage. Most of us track that percentage the way we track our steps: badly, from memory, right before the quarterly check‑in — usually while squinting at a spreadsheet someone built in 2019.

This doesn't do that. It's a small, on‑device calculator that logs your days as you go, does the actual maths the policy is built on, and tells you — plainly, without HR euphemisms — exactly how many more office days you need this month.

Built after one too many Sunday‑night reconciliations. No login. No backend. No one but you ever sees the numbers.

The actual formula
attendance % = days present ÷ eligible working days × 100
eligible days = working days − leave − holidays − comp‑off
Same formula the app runs on your phone. No hidden multiplier, no vibes‑based rounding.

Recovery maths

Below target? It tells you exactly how many more days, out of how many are left, to get back on track — no mental arithmetic required on a Friday afternoon.

05

Notification centre

App errors, update alerts, and a receipt every time you send a bug report — one bell icon, not three toast messages you never saw.

06

Nothing leaves your phone

No login, no backend, no server to breach. Your attendance history is exactly as private as your actual attendance.

07

Office auto-detection

Add every office you use and get a confirm-first banner when you're near any of them — off by default, never on a weekend or over a day you've already logged. Trust the match completely and there's a toggle for that too: switch on auto-confirm and it logs itself the instant you're in range, no tap needed. Not near any of them? It nudges you to log the day yourself instead of quietly saying nothing. Either way, a full-screen "Present — logged" reveal plays once, the moment it's actually true — even if that's hours later, when you pick the phone back up. Plus daily WFH reminders so a work-from-home day never slips through unmarked.

Android is warning me about "unknown sources." Is this safe?

Yes — that warning appears for any app installed outside the Play Store, including this one. Settings → Apps → Special access → Install unknown apps → allow it for your browser or Files app, then install as normal. The app makes no network calls except an optional, anonymous check for new GitHub releases.

How do I install this on iPhone?

Open the app link in Safari — has to be Safari, Chrome and Firefox on iOS don't offer this option. Tap the Share icon, scroll down to "Add to Home Screen," tap Add. It's now a normal-looking app icon: full-screen, no address bar, own name. Always launch it from that icon afterwards, not from a Safari tab — that's what makes the next answer true.

Will my iPhone wipe my data if I don't open it for a while?

Not if you installed it properly (previous question). Regular Safari tabs get their storage wiped by Apple's Intelligent Tracking Prevention after 7 days of inactivity — that's an Apple policy, not a bug here. An app added to the Home Screen runs in its own separate storage that sits outside that sweep, so it's exempt in practice. Apple has never put that exemption in writing, so treat it as very likely rather than guaranteed, and use Settings → Export occasionally if you want a copy that doesn't depend on Apple's storage policy at all.

Why doesn't it notify me on iPhone the way it does on Android?

iOS won't let a locally-installed app push to the lock screen or notification centre without a server sending that push — and standing one up would mean this app suddenly needing a backend and an internet connection it doesn't have today. The in-app reminders still work the moment you open it; you just won't get pinged while it's closed. Android gets the full treatment because it allows notifications to be scheduled locally, no server required.

The app told me an update is out — why doesn't it just update?

Depends which version you're on. The Android app downloads the new APK and hands it to the system installer for you — one tap and a confirm. The web app (iPhone, or Android installed as a home-screen app) can't do that; there's no installer to hand a file to. Instead it fetches the new version quietly in the background, checking automatically every time you open it, and swaps over on its own, usually within a few seconds — Settings → About → Update the app nudges that along. If it's still showing the old version a bit later, fully close it — swipe it away from your recent apps, or close every tab it's open in, not just switch away — and reopen it once. That's the ceiling of what's ever needed: never a reinstall, never removing and re-adding the home-screen icon. Coming from a version installed well before this was added, the very first update after it ships still needs that one manual reopen — a service worker can only start auto-reloading once the page carrying that logic has actually loaded — but every update after that lands on its own. And it never touches your data: an update only replaces the app's own code, kept in a separate storage from your logged days, offices and leave balances. No sign-up either way, before or after — there's never been one.

Why didn't I get an Android notification for an update, even though Settings clearly shows one?

Fixed in 1.5.6. The on-screen "Version X available" text in Settings and the phone's own push notification are two separate things under the hood, and a bug meant one failed push attempt — most likely notification permission not being granted the very first time it silently checked in the background — permanently stopped it from ever trying again for that version, even though the on-screen text kept working correctly the whole time. It now keeps retrying every check until the push actually goes through, without piling up duplicate rows in your in-app notification list.

Why did my Attendance % suddenly look higher than it should?

Fixed in 1.5.7. The app can infer extra "leave taken" days when a reported leave balance drops by more than what's explicitly marked as Leave on the Calendar — useful when leave gets taken without a matching tap on a day. A bug meant it did this even when there was no genuinely-unmarked day left to explain the drop — every weekday already accounted for as Present, WFH, or explicit Leave — quietly shrinking Eligible working days without touching Attended, which inflated the percentage. It now only ever borrows from days that are actually still unmarked, and never more of them than exist. Every past month recalculates on its own — nothing to redo.

Does Plan mode count toward my real percentage?

No, on purpose. Plan mode is a forecast — turn it on for a future month, sketch out Present/WFH/Leave/Comp off day by day, and it shows a separate "Projected" percentage next to your real one. Nothing you plan touches the actual figure until that day arrives and you log it for real, exactly the same way you always have.

How does office auto-detection actually work?

Add your office's coordinates under Settings → Location — the two Bangalore offices, Acacia and Hibiscus, come pre-loaded. When you open the app on a weekday and you're within range, Today shows a banner: "Looks like you're at [office] — mark today as present?" You tap Mark present or Not today. It never marks a day on its own; it only ever offers.

I split time between two offices — does it handle that?

Yes. Every saved office is checked each time, and whichever one you're actually near is the one that gets offered — nothing is fixed to a single location. Add as many as you use. And on a day that matches none of them — a client site, a third office, working from somewhere else entirely — the app doesn't stay silent either: it nudges you with a "log today yourself" banner instead, so a day off the usual map still doesn't slip through unmarked.

Does the app track my location in the background?

No. It checks once, when you open the app — the same way it checks for app updates. There's no background tracking, no location service running while the app is closed, and Android is only ever asked for foreground location permission, never "allow all the time." Close the app and it stops looking entirely.

Where does my location data go?

Nowhere. Your coordinates are compared to the office list you saved, entirely on your own phone, using ordinary geometry — no server, no API call, no map provider involved. The comparison result (a match or not) never leaves the device either.

How do I know GPS is actually working?

Turn on auto-detect and Settings shows a live line under it: permission granted or not, a signal or not, and how far the last reading was from your nearest saved office. It's a one-off check — refreshed when you open Settings, or on tap with "Check GPS now" — not a running tracker, same as auto-detection itself only ever looks once when the app is open.

What if I walk past the office on a Saturday?

Nothing happens. Auto-detection is switched off for every weekend automatically — no exceptions, no setting to override it — so passing near an office off the clock never gets logged as a working day. It also skips any day you've already marked yourself, and only ever asks once per day either way.

Why confirm instead of just logging it automatically?

By default, because "nearby" isn't the same as "at work" — you could be in the building's car park heading somewhere else, dropping something off, or the coordinates could be a little off. A one-tap confirm keeps the ledger accurate without needing to trust GPS blindly, and it means the app never silently rewrites your attendance without you seeing it first — that stays the safer default. If you'd rather it just log itself, Settings → Location has an auto-confirm toggle: turn it on and a match marks the day present the instant it's found, no tap, with the full-screen reveal as your only sign it happened. Every other safeguard — weekdays only, never overriding a day you've already logged, once a day — still applies exactly the same either way.

What's the full-screen animation after a GPS confirm?

A one-time "Present — logged" reveal for the day — a stamp landing with a burst of colour, your office name, and the date. It plays the moment you confirm, and it also catches up if you open the app later: check in at 9am, come back at 9pm, and it still plays once, on that return. It works the same in the installed app and the browser version, and it fires even from the Android recent-apps switcher, not just a fresh launch — because that's the actual point of it. Once a day, then it's done; opening the app again after that shows the ordinary Today screen.

Do the WFH reminders work on iPhone?

Not while the app is closed — same limitation as every other notification in this app on iOS: a browser-installed PWA can't fire a push without a server behind it, and this one deliberately has none. On Android they're two ordinary daily notifications, timed however you set them under Settings → Daily reminders.

Does this connect to any ANZ system?

No. It doesn't touch SuccessFactors, your calendar, badge data, or anything else. You tell it what happened; it does the maths. That's the entire integration surface.

Found a bug?

Report it from inside the app (Settings → About → Report a bug) or open an issue on GitHub. Both reach the same person, at the same speed.
